India as a Union of States

Key Constitutional Topics

Explore essential concepts of Indian Polity

Constitution Overview

The Indian Constitution is the world's longest written constitution, adopted on 26th November 1949 and enacted on 26th January 1950.

Learn More

Fundamental Rights

Part III (Articles 12-35) guarantees six fundamental rights to all citizens: Right to Equality, Freedom, Against Exploitation, Religion, Culture & Education, and Constitutional Remedies.

Learn More

Directive Principles

Part IV (Articles 36-51) contains guidelines for the state to establish a just society. Though not enforceable in court, they are fundamental in governance.

Learn More

Parliament & Executive

The Parliament consists of the President, Lok Sabha, and Rajya Sabha. It's responsible for law-making, while the Executive (President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers) implements laws.

Learn More

Judiciary

The Indian judiciary is independent and integrated, with the Supreme Court at the apex, followed by High Courts and subordinate courts. It safeguards fundamental rights and interprets the Constitution.

Learn More

Elections & Voting

India follows a parliamentary system with universal adult suffrage. The Election Commission of India, an autonomous constitutional authority, administers free and fair elections.
